DATED OFFICIAL GENUS-RANK SENSITIVITY -- FIXED MATRIX, AUDIT ONLY

The dated NCBI taxonomy reconciliation resolves a true genus-rank taxonomic
identifier for 422 of the 426 retained assembly records, spanning 73 official
genus IDs. Four records lack a genus-rank assignment in the retrieved taxonomy
lineage and are explicitly excluded; all four carry the positive nif-derived
label. They are GCF_029919255.1, GCF_003574135.1, GCF_002163975.1 and
GCF_000829235.1. No genus is fabricated from a name token such as Candidatus.
Inputs: taxonomy_genus_audit.csv; raw lineage snapshots and retrieval hashes in
taxonomy_public/taxonomy_batch_*.json and retrieval_manifest.json.

The retained-feature-identical flagged pair is still assigned to different official
genera: MIT S9506 to Synechococcus (tax ID 1129), SS51 to Prochlorococcus (1218).
They remain in different folds in the all-resolved arm. The current-and-resolved
arm excludes suppressed MIT S9506. Official taxonomy alone therefore does not
resolve that sample-identity concern, and its grouping is distinct from the earlier
deliberately conservative connected-genus sensitivity.

Two bounded arms are predeclared: all 422 genus-resolved records (108 positive,
314 negative) and current-assembly plus genus-resolved records (398 records,
103 positive, 295 negative, spanning 70 official genus IDs). The latter excludes
22 currently suppressed records, three previous versions and three additional
current records without genus rank; one unresolved record was already suppressed.
Previous versions are not equated with demonstrated quality failures.

For EACH retained cohort, unique integer official genus IDs are sorted numerically,
then shuffled using np.random.default_rng(42), allocated round-robin to five
folds, and test genomes retain sorted accession order. Numeric order is declared
explicitly; alphabetic string order or the original first-occurrence name order
would yield a different partition. Training/test official genus IDs are disjoint.
The training breadth criterion counts official genus IDs. Exact integer purity
uses 10*k>n and 10*k<9*n among carriers in the training fold. Four classifiers
and two one-predictor Logistic Regression baselines retain original parameters,
training-only scaling and one CPU/BLAS thread. No tuning is performed.

The fixed 2286-family matrix, clustering and global family-prevalence vocabulary
remain unchanged. The frozen 476/981 resource and ranking are not rewritten.
This is a sensitivity to a dated taxonomy/grouping assumption, population
exclusion and repartition together; it cannot isolate a taxonomy-only or
leakage-only effect. It is not whole-genome deduplication or external validation.

Interpretation
The all-resolved mean-fold AUCs are 0.890 RF, 0.821 GB, 0.873 LR and 0.871
XGBoost, with length-only 0.753 and retained-family-count-only 0.813. The
current-and-resolved results are 0.905, 0.804, 0.844 and 0.869, respectively,
with length-only 0.820 and family-count-only 0.808. Family LR exceeds length
in both arms, but its mean-fold difference is only about 0.024 in the latter.
GB falls below both single-size baselines in that latter partition. These are
model-dependent comparisons, not a universal claim of incremental content
discrimination. Unequal fold sizes and taxonomic weights materially affect
the estimand: high fold-mean or pooled-genome AUC alone should not substitute
for the accompanying equal-genus metrics.

The original-OOF same-surviving-row reference is saved with its ORIGINAL fold
assignments, and official-genus weights are applied to that restricted reference
population. Its mean-fold comparison therefore changes partition as well as
fitting; its pooled comparison holds population and weights fixed but still
changes fitting/split assignment. Neither comparison isolates a single mechanism.
No inferential p value is assigned to these dependent, single-seed partitions.
